A kernel that passes brep-conformance and brep-bench in full, Send to slicer as STEP, drag and drop in the browser, and a simpler top bar.

## Downloads

| Platform | File |
|---|---|
| Linux x86_64 (glibc 2.35+: Ubuntu 22.04, Debian 12, Fedora 36 or newer) | `cadrs-v0.2.9-x86_64-linux.tar.gz` |
| Windows x86_64 (Windows 10 or 11) | `cadrs-v0.2.9-x86_64-windows.zip` |
| macOS 11 or newer, Apple silicon and Intel | `cadrs-v0.2.9-universal-macos.dmg` |
| A browser with WebGPU or WebGL2 (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari) | <https://cadrs.net/app/>, or `cadrs-v0.2.9-web.tar.gz` to serve yourself |

The Linux and Windows archives hold the executable, its `libraries/` folder (the built-in symbols, footprints and 3D models), the README and the licenses. Nothing needs installing: unpack it and run `cadrs` (Linux) or `cadrs.exe` (Windows), keeping `libraries/` beside it. `SHA256SUMS.txt` has the files' checksums.

On macOS, open the `.dmg` and drag cadrs to Applications. The app isn't signed with an Apple Developer ID yet, so the first time macOS won't open it: right-click cadrs in Applications and choose **Open**, then **Open** again (on macOS 15 and later, open it once, then choose **Open Anyway** in System Settings › Privacy & Security). Updates on macOS are downloaded from the release page and dragged over the old app.

On Linux, the app needs a Vulkan-capable GPU driver, and ALSA and udev (present on desktop distributions). The Windows build isn't code-signed, so SmartScreen may warn the first time: choose **More info › Run anyway**.

Documents are saved in `~/.local/share/cadrs/` on Linux, `%APPDATA%\cadrs\` on Windows and `~/Library/Application Support/cadrs/` on macOS.

## What's new since v0.2.8

- **Kernel** (#264, #277, #279, #280, #281, #285, #288, #291, #293). cadrs's modelling kernel now passes all 3,609 cases of [brep-conformance](https://github.com/springcraft-ai/brep-conformance)'s current suite (v0.2.8 passed 2,721 of the 3,487 it had then), and on [brep-bench](https://brepbench.org) builds every case but the 3DBenchy (498 passing, none wrong). Booleans of nearly touching or nearly coincident faces, fillets and chamfers on curved and elliptic edges, conic and G2 rounds, offsets, shells, sweeps and STEP round trips are more robust, and most operations are faster: on brep-conformance's operations cadrs takes about a seventh of springrep's time. Where test suites disagree on what an operation should return (a shell too thick to hollow, a torus whose tube crosses its axis), the kernel takes an option instead of picking one convention.
- **Send to slicer as STEP** (#286). Slicers that import STEP (PrusaSlicer, OrcaSlicer, Bambu Studio, SuperSlicer, Creality Print) are sent STEP, so round edges stay round in the print; others get a finely meshed STL instead of one faceted every 15°.
- **Drag and drop in the browser** (#282). Files dropped on <https://cadrs.net/> are imported as on the desktop, the documents page highlights while files are dragged over it, and thumbnails show in the browser.
- **Top bar** (#283). The colour scheme is one button that cycles light, dark and system, and a new AI assistants (MCP) button turns the MCP server on and off and shows its endpoint and port.
- **Gears in the browser** (#263). Gear flanks mesh with a few hundred triangles instead of thousands, so documents with many gears open in the browser without running out of memory.
- **Lofts** (#284). A curve loft with one guide turns a profile drawn the other way again (the Race Car sample builds again).
- **Collaboration** (#275, #276). An editor takes over a shared document when its owner's cadrs quits, not only when the owner closes it, and the browser app can reach a document's owner again.

cadrs is an independent project.
